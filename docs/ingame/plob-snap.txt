$size[14]{$u{Placement Snap}}

While placing a lifted, built or stockpiled object, the placement ghost is pulled flush against the collision boxes of nearby objects - just move the mouse close to something and the ghost snaps against it. No keys needed.

$size[12]{$u{How it behaves}}
* Each axis snaps independently: caught on a wall, the ghost slides smoothly along it while staying flush, and catches the corner when one comes into range.
* Nearby objects you are not touching get their edges lined up with, so rows of cupboards or barrels come out straight.
* Cave walls snap too, even though they are terrain rather than objects.
* A small gap is left on purpose - the server rejects placements that touch exactly. Walls get a slightly larger gap for the same reason.
* Rotating the ghost off the 90-degree grid (Ctrl+scroll) turns snapping off until it is square again.
* While the ghost is snapped, it stops auto-turning to face you, so it stays flush as you slide it.

$size[12]{$u{Controls}}
* Hold $u{Shift} to place smoothly with no snapping at all.
* The checkbox lives in Options > Interface settings ("Snap placement to nearby hitbox edges"). Turning it off restores the vanilla tile-center placement.

$size[12]{$u{Tuning}}
* Console command $font[Monospaced,11]{:placesnap} on its own shows the current grab/release distances.
* $font[Monospaced,11]{:placesnap <grab> <deadzone>} changes how eagerly the snap grabs an edge and how far you must drag to let go again. Defaults are 3.5 and 6.5; a tile is 11 units wide.
* If a placement seems wrong, enable "Placement snap diagnostics" in Options > Debug, place again, and $font[Monospaced,11]{:placesnap} will report what the snap decided and why.
